The Five-Minute Journaling Habit

Here's a pattern I see all the time: someone decides to journal, imagines long candlelit sessions, buys the fancy notebook, and lasts exactly six days. Then the guilt kicks in, and the notebook hides.

Five minutes beats fifty

A five-minute entry, done daily, beats an hour-long entry done monthly. The goal of journaling isn't a beautiful page - it's a habit of noticing. Frequency is the whole game.

The formula that works

Keep it embarrassingly simple:

  1. Anchor it. Attach journaling to something you already do - coffee, brushing teeth, last light.
  2. Use a prompt. Any prompt. Your journal's guided pages do this for you.
  3. Lower the bar. One sentence counts. Some days that's all you'll have, and it's enough.
  4. Forgive misses. Miss two days? Great, start again tonight. Habits are re-won, not lost.

The tiny trigger

For me, it's the alarm on my phone that says "journal" - but the real trick is that I put the journal on the pillow at night, so the morning version of me has no excuse. Put your journal somewhere you cannot ignore it.
